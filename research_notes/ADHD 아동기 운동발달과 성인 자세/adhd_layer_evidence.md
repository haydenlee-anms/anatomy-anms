## Layer 1 — Muscle and connective tissue (strength, muscle size, fibre type, tone, joint hypermobility)

### Takeaway
Maximal strength looks normal in ADHD at the population level and in the one lab study of the hand that measured it. No study measured muscle size, fibre type or muscle tone in ADHD. The case for joint hypermobility in ADHD rests mainly on one small, low-quality clinic study (74% vs 13%). A 2025 population-based study of 11-year-olds found no association. A major complication is that DCD, present in roughly a third to a half of children with ADHD, shows strength/power deficits in some studies. So "normal strength" may hold for ADHD alone but not for ADHD+DCD.

### Cited Findings
**Maximal strength — supports "normal layer 1"**
- Coordinator-provided (not re-verified): Opper et al. 2022 *Sci Rep* (German MoMo cohort, n=2,988, 253 with parent-reported ADHD, followed about 11 years) found no difference in muscular fitness but lower coordination from the first measurement onward. A 2026 three-group fitness study (ADHD n=80 vs ASD vs TD) and Buker et al. 2020 likewise found ADHD muscular fitness / standing broad jump similar to TD. **[moderate]**
- Hotham et al. 2018, *J Neural Transm*, 29 children with ADHD tested **off medication** vs 35 TD: **maximal pinch-grip strength, movement speed and tremor did not differ between groups.** In the same children, grip-force control *did* differ (see Layer 3). The summary did not mention DCD comorbidity handling. (SS) **[weak–moderate]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-017-1822-8); [PubMed 29234901](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29234901)

**Strength in DCD — counter-evidence for the ADHD+DCD subgroup**
- Raynor 2001, *Dev Med Child Neurol* 43(10):676–684, "Strength, power, and coactivation in children with DCD". Later papers cite it as showing **lower peak isokinetic knee flexion/extension torque and lower power** in DCD than TD. I could not see the paper's own abstract (SS). **[weak]** — [Springer review, Curr Dev Disord Rep 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40474-018-0130-9)
- The DCD strength literature is **mixed**. As quoted in a review in the same result set: "Raynor et al. found the knee extensors to be weak, whereas Van der Hoek et al. found no weakness in these muscle groups and Ferguson et al. found these muscles to be stronger for children with DCD." (SS; which review this quote came from is uncertain, either the Springer review above or the PTJ review). **[weak]** — [Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40474-018-0130-9); [PTJ systematic review of field strength tests in DCD](https://academic.oup.com/ptj/article/100/10/1825/5868019)
- Demers et al. 2020, *DMCN*, "Growth and muscle strength development in children with DCD": title found, results not retrieved. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/dmcn.14507)

**Joint hypermobility**
- **Original source of the "74% vs 13%" figure: Shiari R, Saeidifard F, Zahed G. "Evaluation of the prevalence of joint laxity in children with attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder." *Annals of Paediatric Rheumatology* 2013;7:8.** The journal is *Annals of* Paediatric Rheumatology, not *Pediatric Rheumatology*. Design: 86 children with ADHD vs 86 age- and sex-matched controls, aged **3–14 years**. Joint laxity in **74.4% vs 12.8% (p<0.001)**; mean Beighton score reportedly **6/9** in the ADHD group (SS). **[weak]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258509975_Evaluation_of_the_Prevalence_of_Joint_Laxity_in_Children_with_Attention_DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Evaluation-of-the-Prevalence-of-Joint-Laxity-in-Shiari-Saeidifard/641649d335d2fa8dbd613f6ebb2fe37993e699e3)
  - Quality concerns:
    - A later critique (exact source not pinned in the summary) said the "very wide age range ... presents problems in accurately assessing joint hypermobility ... and may have biased the results" (SS).
    - Separately, a 2023 paediatric review notes that a Beighton score **≥6/9 is the cutoff that best identifies GJH at 2 SD above the mean in children**. A group *mean* of 6/9 would therefore be extreme, which is a reason to suspect measurement or threshold issues. — [Orphanet J Rare Dis 2023 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13023-023-02717-2)
- Doğan et al. 2011, *Turk J Rheumatol*, "Benign joint hypermobility syndrome in patients with ADHD": 54 ADHD vs 36 controls. The authors conclude that joint hypermobility "may be associated with ADHD" (SS; prevalence figures not retrieved). **[weak]** — [DOI 10.5606/tjr.2011.029](https://dx.doi.org/10.5606/tjr.2011.029); [full-text PDF link](https://archivesofrheumatology.org/full-text-pdf/412)
- 2024 preliminary study of children with neurodevelopmental disorders, n=102, aged 5–15. **Right-ankle passive ROM was higher in ADHD than in controls (p=0.021).** Beighton scores showed only a *non-significant* trend in ADHD (SS). **[weak]** — [PMC11430528](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11430528/)
- **Counter-evidence:** Glans et al. 2025, *BJPsych Open* 11(6). School-based sample of **223 eleven-year-olds** from 8 schools in western Sweden. Neurodevelopmental problems were assessed with ratings, maternal interview, medical records and academic performance. **High Beighton scores were not associated with neurodevelopmental problems**, unlike findings in adult cohorts. The hypermobile subgroup was small (SS). **[moderate]** for a null in the general population, though underpowered for ADHD specifically. — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/no-association-between-joint-hypermobility-musculoskeletal-pain-and-neurodevelopmental-problems-in-a-schoolbased-sample-of-11yearold-children/F6D550242D1C362CDF89255D47E63136); [Univ. Gothenburg news](https://www.gu.se/en/news/study-finds-no-link-between-joint-hypermobility-pain-and-neurodevelopmental-disorders-in-children-0)
- A 2020 study of children with anxiety disorders, using the same Shiari-Javadi criteria, found ADHD was **not** a predictor of hypermobility syndrome (SS). **[weak]** — [DOAJ, BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2020](https://doaj.org/article/79d5fb7dabae4580a3c1e6ff54144ea4)
- Reviews exist but their content was not retrieved: Baeza-Velasco et al. 2018, "ADHD, joint hypermobility-related disorders and pain: expanding body-mind connections to the developmental age", *ADHD Atten Def Hyp Disord* ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12402-018-0252-2)); "Connective tissue problems and ADHD" 2014 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12402-014-0161-y)).

**Muscle tone / hypotonia**
- **No ADHD-specific study of muscle tone or hypotonia prevalence was found** in two targeted searches.
- The closest is a 2025 Turkish study (32 ADHD vs 36 controls) using the Touwen neurological examination. It found dysfunction in *involuntary movements, associated movements, coordination-balance, fine manipulation and sensory function*. **Tone was not among the domains listed as abnormal** in the excerpt (SS). **[weak]** — [Erzurum repository](https://acikerisim.erzurum.edu.tr/entities/publication/cf93ead9-dda1-4ceb-9584-7d47f73ca6be/full); [Gazi AVESIS](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/f915bb9f-570d-4c8a-b6d3-3b8c9642df86/developmental-coordination-disorder-and-co-occurring-motor-difficulties-in-children-with-attention-deficit-and-hyperactivity-disorder-dikkat-eksikligi-ve-hiperaktivite-bozuklugu-olan-cocuklarda-gelisimsel-koordinasyon-bozuklugu-ve-eslik-eden-motor-gu)
- Clinical tone assessment is inherently subjective: "the subjective nature of the clinical evaluation process makes accurate measurement of muscle tone difficult." — [PMC4275483](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4275483/)

### Inferences
- Maximal force output appears intact in ADHD as a group. Hotham 2018 is the cleanest within-study contrast: normal maximum pinch strength alongside abnormal grip-force control. **This directly supports the "normal strength, impaired control" pattern at the hand.** [moderate overall for "normal max strength in ADHD"]
- For an individual with ADHD **plus** DCD-level motor problems, the DCD strength literature means layer 1 cannot be assumed normal. Lower power and strength are reported in some DCD studies, but results conflict. [weak]
- Hypermobility could plausibly affect posture through passive joint stability and the effort needed to hold positions. However, the paediatric ADHD evidence is **weak and conflicting**. The 74% figure comes from a small Iranian clinic sample with a 3–14 age span, published in a low-visibility journal, with blinding not reported. The best-designed paediatric study (Glans 2025) is null. I would not present hypermobility as an established ADHD feature in children. [speculative link to posture]
- "Low muscle tone" is often mentioned clinically in ADHD/DCD, but I found **no quantitative paediatric ADHD data**. It should be reported as an evidence gap, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No ADHD studies of muscle cross-sectional area, fibre type, muscle stiffness (myotonometry or shear-wave elastography) or objectively measured tone were found.
- Doğan 2011 prevalence numbers were not retrieved.
- **Leads not verified this session (from prior knowledge, may be inaccurate):** Kindgren et al. 2021 (Sweden, GJH in a child neuropsychiatric clinic, *Neuropsychiatr Dis Treat*); Csecs et al. 2022 *BMJ Open* (adults; the coordinator's secondary source); Glans et al. 2021/2022 (adult ADHD and GJH).
- Raynor 2001: the actual coactivation and strength data and effect sizes were not retrieved.
- Unclear whether Glans 2025 had enough children with diagnosed ADHD to test the ADHD association specifically.

## Layer 2 — Neural drive (motor unit recruitment and firing, voluntary activation, EMG)

### Takeaway
**No ADHD study of voluntary activation (twitch interpolation), motor unit recruitment or firing, or EMG at maximal effort was found.** This layer is essentially unmeasured in ADHD. The only motor-unit-level paediatric data come from DCD: one tiny HD-EMG study (9 vs 9). It found **normal mean firing rates** but **less variable** firing across contractions, which the authors read as "more fixed" strategies.

### Cited Findings
- Esselaar et al. 2023, *Front Hum Neurosci*: 9 DCD vs 9 TD. HD-EMG of the first dorsal interosseous during handgrip at **10 ± 5% MVC**, decomposed into motor unit spike trains.
  - **Mean firing rate: TD 7.74 ± 2.16 pps vs DCD 7.86 ± 2.39 pps (no difference)**, even though the DCD group was older.
  - **Across-contraction variability was lower in DCD:** SD of the inter-pulse-interval CoV was **0.28 ± 0.11 vs 0.38 ± 0.12 in TD**. The authors called this "surprising" and proposed a possible neuromuscular biomarker.
  - Few other group differences remained once force steadiness was controlled (SS).
  - **[weak]** — [PMC10740205](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10740205/); [PubMed 38144895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38144895/); [MMU e-space PDF](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/633624/1/Children%20with%20developmental%20coordination%20disorder%20have%20less%20variable%20motor%20unit%20firing%20rate%20characteristics%20across%20contracti.pdf)
- Hotham 2018 (ADHD, off medication): normal maximum pinch strength and normal tremor (SS). This is indirect evidence that peak drive to hand muscles is not grossly reduced. **[weak]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-017-1822-8)
- Gilbert et al. 2019, *Neurology* 93(6):e599–e610: resting M1 excitability **increased** during engagement in a response-inhibition task (p<0.0001). Excerpt wording was ambiguous about whether this was a group effect (SS). Corticospinal excitability is not neural drive per se, but it is the nearest TMS measure. **[weak for this layer]** — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007899)
- Christ et al. 2025 (coordinator-provided; re-confirmed in search): children with ADHD produced **higher** joint torques during balance (ankle +22% dynamic / +20% static; upper body +11% / +7.5%) but **lower hip torque**. n=17 vs 24. The authors interpret this as reduced precision of muscle activation, i.e. "more output, less precisely graded" (SS). **[weak]** — [PubMed 40822294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40822294/); [PMC12354385](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12354385/); [Semantic Scholar PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/0aae/8a89a9510f62741b6d19ccfeeaa649b87798.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Layer 2 is normal in ADHD" is **untested, not supported**. The working hypothesis should say "no evidence of reduced maximal drive" rather than "neural drive is normal." [speculative]
- The DCD motor-unit result does not show reduced drive. It shows a less flexible pattern across repetitions, which fits better under "control" (layer 3) than "drive". [speculative]
- The higher balance torques in ADHD (Christ 2025) suggest drive during submaximal postural tasks may be *excessive or poorly graded* rather than deficient. If anything, this points against a "weak/floppy" model. [speculative]

### Gaps
- Searched without success for ADHD studies of twitch interpolation, motor unit decomposition, or surface-EMG amplitude/onset during MVC. The planned searches "EMG ADHD children" and "cocontraction ADHD" were not run because the search budget ran out.
- No study separates central drive from peripheral muscle properties in ADHD.

## Layer 3 — Force control (steadiness and variability, coactivation, rate of force development, grading of submaximal force)

### Takeaway
Several small studies agree that **children with ADHD show more variable submaximal grip force and poorer anticipatory scaling of grip force, while maximal pinch strength is normal.** This is the most direct evidence for the "normal strength, impaired control" hypothesis. In the oldest study, however, the variability concentrated in ADHD children *with* movement problems (a DCD-like subgroup). DCD shows "equal force, more variability" in one study but **no CoV difference** in another. No ADHD data exist on coactivation or rate of force development.

### Cited Findings
- **Hotham et al. 2018, *J Neural Transm*** (29 ADHD off medication vs 35 TD):
  - Children with ADHD **lifted a novel object with lower grip force (p=0.036)** and **held it with more variable grip force (p=0.003)**.
  - Movement speed, maximal pinch strength and tremor were unaffected.
  - Finger-tapping rhythm was weaker, and MABC-2 manual dexterity and aiming-and-catching scores were poorer.
  - Authors: "ADHD is associated with deficits in multiple, but not all domains of upper limb function." Effect sizes were not retrieved (SS).
  - **[weak–moderate]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-017-1822-8); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29234901)
- **Pereira et al. 2000, *Dev Med Child Neurol*, "Detrimental neural control of precision grip lifts in children with ADHD"** (25 boys with ADHD, split by presence of movement dysfunction; controls included):
  - "Variability of motor performance was predominant in the ADHD+ [motor-problem] group." Several showed **higher** grip force during gripping.
  - They had difficulty adapting motor output to different weights, which the authors read as "deficient anticipatory parameter control based on memory representations" (SS).
  - **[weak]** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-8749.2000.tb00711.x); [PubMed 10981933](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10981933/)
  - Note that the direction of force error differs between studies: higher grip force here, lower lift force in Hotham 2018.
- Same group, DCD and DAMP children: "Parametric control of fingertip forces during precision grip lifts in children with DCD and DAMP", *Neuropsychologia* (title only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393200001329)
- 2023 *Front Hum Neurosci* study, 13 ADHD vs 12 TD, static grip-force task with eye tracking: the ADHD group showed **greater variability of both gaze and grip force** and **less gaze–force synchronization** than TD (SS; authors and effect sizes not retrieved). **[weak]** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2023.1294931/full)
- Developmental context: in TD children aged 5–12, force CoV matures mainly up to about age 9–10, which has been linked to corticospinal maturation (SS). If a maturational-lag model holds, group differences may be age-dependent. — [ScienceDirect, "Development of isometric force and force control in children"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0926641003000818)
- **DCD — supporting:** Smits-Engelsman, Westenberg & Duysens 2008, *Hum Mov Sci*, "Children with DCD are **equally able to generate force but show more variability** than typically developing children." Younger children with DCD were more affected than older ones, which suggests coping strategies develop for increased "stochastic variability", especially at higher force levels (SS). **[weak–moderate]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167945708000171); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5492939_Children_with_developmental_coordination_disorder_are_equally_able_to_generate_force_but_show_more_variability_than_typically_developing_children)
  - Related framing paper: Smits-Engelsman & Wilson 2013, *DMCN*, "Noise, variability, and motor performance in DCD" (title only). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/dmcn.12311)
- **DCD — counter:** at low-force handgrip (10% MVC), one DCD study, most likely Esselaar 2023 (attribution inferred), found **"no differences in the force variability (CoV) nor in the accuracy between the DCD group and the TD group"** (SS). **[weak]** — [PMC10740205](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10740205/)
- Young adults with ADHD (persistence-relevant): McCracken et al. 2023, *Front Hum Neurosci*, 15 ADHD vs 15 controls, thumb force-matching at 2–12% MVC with median-nerve SEPs. **Both groups improved and kept the gain at 24 h retention.** The summary reported no group difference in learning. A companion source-localization paper found increased BA31/precuneus activation post-learning in ADHD (SS). **[weak]** counter-evidence: force grading is learnable and not grossly impaired in young adults. — [PMC9849696](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9849696/); [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2022.1078925/full); [companion paper, Brain Sci 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci13020370)
- Balance torques: Christ 2025 (above) shows **excess torque at the ankle and upper body with lower hip torque** during balance. This is a postural force-grading abnormality. **[weak]** — [PubMed 40822294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40822294/)
- **Coactivation:** Raynor 2001 (DCD) examined coactivation, but the data were not retrieved. **No ADHD coactivation study was found.** — [Springer review citing Raynor](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40474-018-0130-9)
- **Rate of force development:** no ADHD or DCD RFD study was found. A DCD study on "lower limb muscle reflex contraction latency and peak force" linked to postural control appeared in results (title only). — [Medicine (Baltimore) via Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/md-journal/fulltext/10.1097/md.0000000000001785~deficits-in-lower-limb-muscle-reflex-contraction-latency-and)

### Inferences
- Layer 3 is the **best-supported "impaired control" layer**, but the evidence is thin: three small grip studies (n=13–29 per group) and one balance-torque study (n=17). It is consistent in direction (more variable force) and inconsistent in the direction of mean force error. [weak–moderate]
- Pereira 2000 suggests the force-control deficit may track **motor-problem (DCD-like) status rather than ADHD per se**. Whether ADHD *without* DCD shows force-variability deficits is unresolved. [speculative]
- Raised intra-individual variability is a hallmark of ADHD in reaction-time tasks; Gilbert 2019 also found go RTs more variable (p=0.002). Some force variability may therefore reflect fluctuating attention rather than a neuromuscular control deficit. None of the grip studies retrieved separated these. [speculative]

### Gaps
- No ADHD study of isometric force steadiness (CoV at fixed %MVC) with an adequate sample, of coactivation (agonist–antagonist EMG), or of RFD.
- Effect sizes for Hotham 2018 and the 2023 grip–gaze study were not retrieved.
- Unclear whether Hotham 2018 screened for or stratified by DCD.

## Layer 4 — Coordination and motor skills (fine/gross motor, balance/postural control, handwriting, DCD comorbidity, proprioception, motor learning, motor timing)

### Takeaway
Many studies agree that children with ADHD as a group score worse on standardized motor batteries, and that **roughly 30–55% meet DCD-level criteria**. Problems track **inattention** more than hyperactivity. The DCD postural literature is meta-analysed, with large clinical-test effects (SMD 1.63) and moderate sway effects (SMD 0.55). For ADHD, postural control data are small-sample and partly conflicting. **Handwriting quality/legibility** deficits are consistent, while speed is not. **Implicit procedural sequence learning appears preserved in ADHD** (meta-analysis), which counts against a global "motor learning" deficit. In DCD, the leading account is an **internal-model / predictive-control deficit**. Timing variability is elevated.

### Cited Findings
**DCD prevalence in ADHD (also see the dedicated section below)**
- Coordinator-provided: Kaiser et al. 2015 *Res Dev Disabil* — more than 50% of children with ADHD score poorly on motor tests; motor impairment prevalence is **8–73% depending on the tool**; the inattentive subtype shows more fine-motor and reaction-time problems.
- Pitcher, Piek & Hay 2003, *DMCN* 45(8):525–535:
  - Boys only: ADHD-PI n=50, ADHD-HI n=16, ADHD-C n=38 vs 39 controls; MABC and Purdue Pegboard.
  - ADHD boys had worse movement ability, and a "high percentage" had DCD-consistent difficulties. MABC total impairment was **less severe in ADHD-HI** than in the other two subtypes.
  - A secondary South African review gives motor-problem rates of about **58% PI, 49% C, 47% HI**, attributing them to "Piek & Pitcher", so attribution to the 2003 paper is uncertain (SS).
  - **[moderate]** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1469-8749.2003.tb00952.x); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/developmental-medicine-and-child-neurology/article/abs/fine-and-gross-motor-ability-in-males-with-adhd/154381A799759217AD9A2DE3215ADBED); [SA review](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S2310-38332018000300004)

**Fine motor / upper limb**
- Hotham 2018: poorer MABC-2 manual dexterity and aiming-and-catching; weaker tapping rhythm (SS). **[weak–moderate]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-017-1822-8)
- Flapper et al. 2006, *DMCN*, "Fine motor skills and effects of methylphenidate in children with ADHD and DCD" (title only; results not retrieved). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0012162206000375)

**Handwriting ("slow hand development")**
- Systematic review (~2024), "Is There a Deficit in Product and Process of Handwriting in Children with ADHD?": 21 articles.
  - **All (100%) methodologically satisfactory studies reported impaired product (quality/legibility).**
  - **91.7% reported abnormal process** (kinematics).
  - **Only 25% found a difference in speed.**
  - Methods varied widely (SS).
  - **[moderate]** — [PMC10813961](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10813961/)
- Small study, 16 children with ADHD symptoms vs 16 controls (grades 4–5): writing speed differed **only under verbal working-memory load**, where the ADHD group wrote more slowly with greater intra-individual variability (SS; exact citation not pinned — it appeared among results that included PubMed 29366923). **[weak]** — [PubMed 29366923](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29366923/)
- "Handwriting performance and underlying factors in children with ADHD", *Res Dev Disabil* 2012 (title only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0891422212000388)

**Balance / postural control — ADHD**
- **No ADHD-specific meta-analysis of postural control was found** (two searches). The Christ 2025 paper cites broader motor meta-analyses (Amiri et al. 2017; Ruiz-Goikoetxea et al. 2018) that I could not retrieve (SS).
- Shum & Pang 2009, *J Pediatr*: **43 ADHD-combined vs 50 age- and sex-matched TD**, Sensory Organization Test. ADHD scored lower on:
  - **composite equilibrium −10.3% (p<.001)**
  - **somatosensory ratio −2.1% (p=.029)**
  - **vestibular ratio −15.6% (p=.037)**
  - **visual ratio −16.0% (p=.001)**
  - (SS) **[moderate–weak]** — [PubMed 19446843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19446843/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24431775_Children_with_Attention_Deficit_Hyperactivity_Disorder_Have_Impaired_Balance_Function_Involvement_of_Somatosensory_Visual_and_Vestibular_Systems)
- Bucci et al. (~2016), "Postural instability in children with ADHD is improved by methylphenidate": centre-of-pressure spatial and temporal measures were poorer in ADHD than in sex-, age- and IQ-matched TD. **Methylphenidate improved postural performance, especially when visual or proprioceptive cues were misleading** (SS). **[weak–moderate]**; it also shows a **medication confound**. — [PMC4854903](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4854903/)
- **Counter-evidence:** *Res Dev Disabil* 2012, "Children with ADHD show **no deficits in plantar foot sensitivity and static balance** compared to healthy controls" (title only; sample not retrieved). **[weak–moderate]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0891422212001382)
- A review summary notes ADHD children have poorer balance control "especially at complex balance tasks, e.g. with sensory input reduction (eyes closed)" (SS), which reconciles the null static-balance finding with positive findings in harder conditions. — [ResearchGate (Shum & Pang page)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24431775_Children_with_Attention_Deficit_Hyperactivity_Disorder_Have_Impaired_Balance_Function_Involvement_of_Somatosensory_Visual_and_Vestibular_Systems)
- Christ 2025: higher ankle and upper-body torques, lower hip torque (see above). **[weak]** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40822294/)
- Exercise-intervention meta-analyses in ADHD: long-term exercise improves motor skills overall (three-level meta-analysis **g=0.72, 95% CI 0.31–1.14**). One synthesis reports **no significant gain in balance or flexibility** (SS). **[moderate]** that motor skills are trainable. — [BMC Pediatr 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12887-025-05857-5); [ADHD Evidence blog summary](https://www.adhdevidence.org/blog/can-certain-types-of-physical-activity-improve-motor-skills-in-children-and-adolescents-with-adhd); [Zhang et al. 2026, Clin Rehabil](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02692155261428944)

**Balance / postural control — DCD (meta-analysed)**
- **Verbecque et al. 2021, *Gait & Posture* 83:268–279**: 31 studies (1,152 DCD, 1,103 TD, mean age 10.4), 17 meta-analysed.
  - **MABC balance subscale SMD 1.63 (95% CI 1.30–1.97).**
  - **Eyes-closed bipedal sway SMD 0.55 (0.32–0.78).**
  - Direction-specific reductions in limits of stability, and **delayed anticipatory postural adjustments** (SS).
  - **[strong–moderate]** — [Maastricht CRIS](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/balance-control-in-individuals-with-developmental-coordination-di/); [UHasselt PDF](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/bitstream/1942/33034/2/Balance%20in%20DCD_2020.pdf)
- DCD reaching: an abstract describes **delayed activation of anterior trunk muscles, with anticipatory activation absent in 3 of 4 anterior trunk muscles**. The authors suggest this may cause "poor proximal stability and consequently poor arm movement control." The search summary attributed this to Raynor 2001; I suspect it is actually Johnston et al. 2002 (*Hum Mov Sci*). **Attribution unverified** (SS). **[weak]** — [Springer review context](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40474-018-0130-9)

**Proprioception / kinesthesia**
- **No study directly measuring joint position sense or kinesthesia in children with ADHD was found.** Indirect evidence is mixed:
  - Shum & Pang's somatosensory ratio was only slightly lower (−2.1%, p=.029).
  - The 2012 plantar-sensitivity study was null.
  - Bucci's MPH effect was greatest under misleading proprioceptive or visual cues (SS).
  - **[weak]** — [PubMed 19446843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19446843/); [ScienceDirect 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0891422212001382); [PMC4854903](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4854903/)
- DCD: a 2025 reliability study of joint position sense and force sense in children with DCD is described as "the first systematic evaluation" of the reliability of these methods. This implies proprioceptive measurement in DCD is still methodologically immature (SS). — [PMC12821486](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12821486/)

**Motor learning — procedural / implicit sequence learning**
- **Meta-analysis (2020, *Front Psychol* 11:560064), "Procedural Sequence Learning in ADHD: A Meta-Analysis"**: 7 studies; **procedural sequence learning appears preserved in ADHD**. Heterogeneity was significant and partly explained by participant age.
  - Author attribution: the search summary said "Ebisch". I believe the authors are Sanjeevan, Cardy & Anagnostou, but this is **unverified**.
  - (SS) **[moderate]** — **counter-evidence to a global layer-4 learning deficit.** — [PubMed 33192824](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33192824/); [Patrinum record](https://patrinum.ch/record/339313?ln=en); [Frontiers XML](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.560064/xml)
- Primary study (alternating SRT; children with ADHD, Tourette, TS+ADHD, TD): **all four groups learned the sequence and did not differ** (SS; author not pinned, possibly Takács et al.). **[weak–moderate]** — cited in the same search results as the meta-analysis
- Measurement caveat: the SRT task's test-retest reliability for the procedural learning effect is **r < 0.40**, well below psychometric standards (SS). This argues for caution with individual-difference claims. — [PMC10354485](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10354485/); [R Soc Open Sci](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/10/7/221542/91892/The-reliability-of-the-serial-reaction-time-task)
- Contrast: procedural learning **is** impaired in dyslexia by SRT meta-analysis. — [PMC3784964](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3784964/)
- Young adults with ADHD learned a novel force-matching task as well as controls and retained it at 24 h (SS). **[weak]** — [PMC9849696](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9849696/)

**Motor learning — internal-model deficit hypothesis (DCD)**
- Adams, Lust, Wilson & Steenbergen 2014, *Neurosci Biobehav Rev* 47:225–244, "Compromised motor control in children with DCD: A deficit in the internal model? — A systematic review" (doi 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2014.08.011). Later work cites it as supporting **internal-modelling (predictive/feed-forward control) deficit as a common underlying problem in DCD** (SS; abstract not retrieved). **[moderate]** — cited via [PubMed 37926854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37926854) and [Adams et al. 2017, Br J Psychol](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjop.12183)
- Subara-Zukic et al. 2022, *Front Psychol* 13:809455, combined systematic review and meta-analysis. As cited by later papers: poorer inhibitory control, working memory and executive attention in DCD, and difficulty integrating predictive sensory information (visual, proprioceptive, vestibular) for balance, which increases sway and gait variability (SS; effect sizes not retrieved). **[moderate]** — [PMC8829815](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8829815/); [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.809455/pdf); [Frontiers 2024 citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1279427/full)
- Wilson et al. 2013 meta-analysis, "Understanding performance deficits in DCD: a meta-analysis of recent research" (title only). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232736586_Understanding_performance_deficits_in_developmental_coordination_disorder_A_meta-analysis_of_recent_research)

**Motor timing**
- Puyjarinet et al. 2017, *Sci Rep*, "Children and adults with ADHD cannot move to the beat": higher motor variability when tapping without sound, and **lower synchronization even after covarying motor variability**, in both children and adults (SS). **[moderate]**; relevant to persistence. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11295-w)
- Neurology 2012 conference abstract: children with ADHD were "marginally slower and significantly more variable" on finger-tapping sequences. **[weak]** — [Neurology abstract P07.144](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.78.1_supplement.P07.144)
- **Counter / mixed (adults):** prior studies "failed to show a difference in spontaneous finger tapping rate and ... inconsistent findings regarding finger tapping variability". One adult study found sensorimotor synchronization "highly accurate ... similar to controls" (SS). **[weak]** — [bioRxiv 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.24.887802v2.full)
- fMRI timing meta-analysis in ADHD exists (brain activation, not behaviour). — [ScienceDirect, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763412001285)

### Inferences
- Layer 4 deficits at the group level are **well established** [strong for "ADHD groups score lower on motor batteries"]. But a large share of that signal likely comes from the **DCD-comorbid subgroup**: prevalence is 30–55%, and problems link to inattention.
- The pattern within layer 4 is **not uniformly impaired**:
  - Implicit sequence learning is preserved.
  - Force-matching learning is preserved in adults.
  - Static balance is null in at least one study.
  - Handwriting *speed* is inconsistent.
  - Deficits concentrate in **variability, legibility/quality, timing, and balance under sensory challenge**.
  - This fits "noisy execution / attention-dependent control" better than "cannot learn movements". [speculative]
- For the client's experience of "difficulty learning movements", the DCD internal-model / predictive-control account fits better than the ADHD procedural-learning literature. This matters for whether the person had DCD-level problems. [speculative]
- Delayed anticipatory postural adjustments and delayed trunk muscle activation (DCD) are the most plausible mechanistic bridge from childhood motor problems to posture: poor proximal/trunk stability. **There is no longitudinal evidence that this produces lasting changes in static alignment.** [speculative]

### Gaps
- No ADHD postural-control meta-analysis was found. Amiri et al. 2017 and Ruiz-Goikoetxea et al. 2018 were not retrieved.
- No ADHD proprioception (joint position sense or kinesthesia) study was found.
- No ADHD visuomotor adaptation study was retrieved (search not run because the budget ran out).
- **Leads not verified this session (prior knowledge, may be inaccurate):**
  - Flapper et al. 2006: methylphenidate improved handwriting/fine motor in ADHD+DCD, but a portion stayed impaired.
  - Bart et al. 2010 and Jacobi-Polishook et al. 2009: MPH and balance in ADHD.
  - Goulardins et al. 2015/2017: motor profile reviews.
  - Kofler et al. 2013 meta-analysis of reaction-time variability.
  - Noreika et al. 2013 timing review.
  - Brossard-Racine et al.: handwriting/visuomotor in ADHD.

## Prevalence of DCD in ADHD, and how ADHD+DCD differs from ADHD alone

### Takeaway
Across clinical and community samples, **about 30–55% of children with ADHD show DCD-level motor problems**, versus about 5–6% (range 4–19%) in the general population. Motor problems are linked to **inattentive** rather than hyperactive symptoms. Parents still report them in adolescence. Direct ADHD+DCD vs ADHD-only comparisons were thin in what I could retrieve. The one direct physiological contrast (Pereira 2000) placed the force-variability deficit in the motor-problem subgroup.

### Cited Findings
- Dutch IMAGE-related study (probably Fliers et al. 2008, *J Neural Transm*; year and venue unverified): **486 children with ADHD vs 269 controls**, parent DCDQ and teacher ratings.
  - Motor coordination problems in **about one third** of ADHD.
  - Related to **inattentive rather than hyperactive/impulsive** symptoms.
  - Parents still reported problems in teenagers, while teacher-reported prevalence diminished somewhat with age (SS).
  - **[moderate]** — [VU Amsterdam research portal](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/motor-coordination-problems-in-children-and-adolescents-with-adhd-2/)
- IMAGE GWAS of DCDQ in 313 Dutch children with ADHD: 15 SNPs nominally associated, none genome-wide significant (SS). **[weak]**; shows motor problems treated as a heritable dimension within ADHD. — [Heidelberg catalogue](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1571747362)
- Prospective study, 96 children with ADHD: **DCD in 55.2%, more in the inattentive type** (SS; probably Watemberg et al. 2007, *DMCN*, unverified). **[moderate]** — via [PMC8000379](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8000379/)
- Spanish case-control study: **75% probable DCD** in a small ADHD group (n=20) (SS). **[weak]** — via [PMC8000379](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8000379/)
- Hong Kong 2016 (95 children with ADHD): 63 had no motor problems, 15 borderline, 17 definite. The authors estimate up to one third may have DCD. Their review cites 30–50% DCD in ADHD depending on definitions, and 4–19% DCD in the general population (SS). **[moderate]** — [HKMJ PDF](https://www.hkmj.org/system/files/hkmj154591.pdf)
- Brazil (São Paulo, MABC-2): **43.38%** of ADHD students showed indications of DCD vs **0%** of students with good academic performance (SS). **[weak–moderate]** — [SIDALC/SciELO](https://www.sidalc.net/search/Record/oai:scielo:S2526-89102018000300590)
- Turkey 2025 (32 ADHD vs 36 controls): children with ADHD were more likely to have DCD (p<0.05). Touwen examination showed dysfunction in involuntary and associated movements, coordination-balance, fine manipulation and sensory function (SS). **[weak]** — [Erzurum repository](https://acikerisim.erzurum.edu.tr/entities/publication/cf93ead9-dda1-4ceb-9584-7d47f73ca6be/full)
- Windsor group (Deasley et al. 2018) cites **30–50%** co-occurrence (SS). — [Univ. Windsor PDF](https://www.uwindsor.ca/psychology/child-neuropsychology-research-group/sites/uwindsor.ca.psychology.child-neuropsychology-research-group/files/deasley_et_al_2018_02_11_dcd_adhd.pdf)
- A 2025 German-speaking parent survey (n=168 families) reports findings in line with the "frequently estimated global co-occurrence rate of 50%" (SS). — [Mannheim PDF](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/68763/1/s40359-024-02282-8.pdf)
- Framing debate: "Is the motor impairment in ADHD a co-occurring deficit or a phenotypic characteristic?" (2020 review; title only). Motor coordination is **not a diagnostic feature of ADHD** in current manuals (SS). — [Springer, Adv Neurodev Disord 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41252-020-00159-6)
- Shared aetiology: twin and genetic work on DCD and ADHD (title only). — [ScienceDirect, Hum Mov Sci 2005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167945705000837); [Expert Rev Neurother 2010](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1586/ern.10.20)
- ADHD+motor problems vs ADHD alone (physiology): Pereira 2000 found grip-force variability "predominant in the ADHD+ group" (SS). **[weak]** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-8749.2000.tb00711.x)

### Inferences
- An adult with lifelong "very poor sports ability and difficulty learning movements" plus ADHD fits the **ADHD+DCD subgroup** better than typical ADHD. Many children with ADHD (roughly half or more) do *not* have DCD-level problems. [speculative but well-grounded in the prevalence data]
- Prevalence estimates vary roughly 8–73% (Kaiser) because of tool and cutoff differences: questionnaire (DCDQ) vs performance (MABC), and 5th vs 15th percentile cutoffs. The report should state the definition behind any figure it quotes. [moderate]

### Gaps
- No retrieved study directly compared **posture or balance** in ADHD+DCD vs ADHD-only vs DCD-only.
- **Leads not verified (prior knowledge):**
  - Tseng et al. 2004 (ADHD with and without DCD, motor/attention).
  - Fliers et al. 2009/2010 (undertreatment of motor problems in ADHD; participation).
  - Gillberg's DAMP cohort: Rasmussen & Gillberg 2000, *JAACAP*, long-term outcome to age 22, reportedly worse outcomes for ADHD+DCD.
  - Kooistra et al. 2005 (motor correlates in ADHD/DCD/RD).

## Layer 5 — Inhibitory circuits (SICI, interhemispheric inhibition, mirror movements/overflow, neurological soft signs)

### Takeaway
**Reduced SICI in children with ADHD is replicated** by the Cincinnati/Kennedy Krieger group (2011, 2019) and is reliable on retest (ICC >0.75). It correlates with symptom severity and motor soft signs, but it is **not specific to ADHD**. Excessive **mirror overflow** declines with age but does not fully resolve by adolescence. The sex difference is **disputed**: earlier work found it in boys only, while a later study found similar overflow in boys and girls. Neurological soft signs (PANESS) are markedly elevated, but no NSS meta-analysis was found.

### Cited Findings
**SICI and TMS**
- Gilbert et al. 2011, *Neurology*, "Motor cortex inhibition: a marker of ADHD behavior and motor development in children": **49 ADHD vs 49 age-matched controls, aged 8–12, all right-handed**. **Mean SICI reduced by 40% (p<0.0001).** Coordinator-provided, not re-verified: r=−0.52 with severity; PANESS 59% worse. **[moderate]** — [PubMed 21321335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21321335/); [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0b013e31820c2ebd); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/737530)
- Gilbert et al. 2019, *Neurology* 93(6):e599–e610 (sample size not retrieved):
  - ADHD showed **less SICI at rest (p=0.02), during go (p=0.03) and during stop trials (p=0.02)**.
  - Go responses were **slower (p=0.01) and more variable (p=0.002)** in ADHD (SS).
  - **[moderate]** (replication). — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007899)
- 2025 *Clin Neurophysiol* reliability study, 64 children with ADHD aged 8–12: **SICI test-retest ICC >0.75 (good)**; ICF ICC >0.50 (moderate). This supports SICI as a candidate biomarker. The paper describes reduced SICI as "a consistent finding in children with ADHD" (SS). **[moderate]** — [PubMed 40411982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40411982/); [PMC12250949](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12250949/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1388245725006029)
- **Caveats:** reduced SICI is "not specific for ADHD", also occurring in Parkinson's disease and OCD. Moll et al. reported that **methylphenidate improves** SICI, so medication status matters (SS). — [Psychiatria Danubina 2016 conference paper](https://www.psychiatria-danubina.com/UserDocsImages/pdf/dnb_vol28_sup1/dnb_vol28_sup1_99.pdf)
- Task-related modulation: in ADHD, motor cortex up-modulation during response inhibition is reduced (SS). — [PMC13359149](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13359149/)
- **No SICI meta-analysis in paediatric ADHD was found.**

**Mirror overflow**
- 2025 study (PMC13359276), "Quantifying age-related changes in mirror overflow in children and adolescents with ADHD": overflow in ADHD youth **diminishes into adolescence but does not resolve completely**, a "developmentally resolving effect and a somewhat persistent difference". The authors call for longitudinal samples (SS; author list not confirmed in the summary). **[moderate]** — [PMC13359276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13359276); [PubMed 42563946](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42563946/)
- Earlier Mostofsky-group work, "Sex effects on mirror overflow during finger tapping in children with ADHD", *JINS*: **boys with ADHD showed more phasic and total overflow than TD boys; girls with ADHD did not differ from TD girls** (SS). **[weak–moderate]** — [Cambridge JINS](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-neuropsychological-society/article/abs/sex-effects-on-mirror-overflow-during-finger-tapping-in-children-with-adhd/1A4A833B9233EB31E13AAE373D84AB61); [JHU Pure](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/sex-effects-on-mirror-overflow-during-finger-tapping-in-children-/)
- **Counter-evidence on sex:** Chen et al. 2022 (Mostofsky co-author), ages 5–12: **boys and girls with ADHD showed similar excessive overflow.** Boys, but not girls, showed more variable tap timing than TD. The authors attribute the discrepancy to the broader age range (SS). **[weak–moderate]** — [Frontiers Child Adolesc Psychiatry 2025 (context)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/child-and-adolescent-psychiatry/articles/10.3389/frcha.2025.1582502/full); [PMC12222223 sex-differences review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12222223/)
- "Quantifying excessive mirror overflow in children with ADHD" (MacNeil et al. 2011, *Neurology*; title only). — [PubMed 21321336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21321336/)
- Interhemispheric mechanism: "Increased interhemispheric somatomotor functional connectivity and mirror overflow in ADHD", *NeuroImage: Clinical* 2021 (title only). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213158221002035)

**Neurological soft signs**
- India (*Indian J Psychiatry* 2012), 52 children with ADHD: **NSS present in 84%** (PANESS), equally across inattentive and hyperactive types (SS). **[weak]** (no control group in excerpt). — [Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/indianjpsychiatry/fulltext/10.4103/0019-5545.99540~neurological-soft-signs-in-children-with-attention-deficit); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230872036_Neurological_soft_signs_in_children_with_attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder)
- Thai university-hospital cross-sectional study, ages 6–18, 24 ADHD vs 24 controls: **median PANESS 35 vs 8.5** (higher = worse) (SS). **[weak]** — [JMUST 2018](https://www.jmust.org/elib/journal/doi/10.35460/2546-1621.2018-0104/full)
- *Hum Brain Mapp* (2024/25), "Subtle motor signs in children with ADHD and their white matter correlates": **higher PANESS total associated with lower FC within bilateral corticospinal tract** (SS). **[weak–moderate]** — [Wiley HBM](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hbm.70002)
- A quantitative evaluation system for NSS in ADHD exists (methods). — [PMC4732149](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4732149/)
- **No pooled NSS meta-analysis for paediatric ADHD was found.**

### Inferences
- Layer 5 is the **best-replicated physiological difference** in ADHD: SICI is replicated, reliable, and tied to severity and soft signs. Together with overflow, it supports the "impaired control" side of the hypothesis. [moderate]
- These are markers of **immature or reduced cortical inhibition** that largely track development, with overflow declining with age. Their direct link to adult posture is unstudied. [speculative]
- Medication can change SICI and posture (methylphenidate), so studies mixing medicated and unmedicated children may underestimate or blur differences. [moderate]

### Gaps
- **Interhemispheric inhibition (ipsilateral silent period, paired-pulse IHI) in ADHD was not searched** because the budget ran out. Leads from prior knowledge, not verified: Buchmann et al. 2003 and Garvey et al. 2005 reported altered or immature transcallosal inhibition in ADHD.
- No SICI or NSS meta-analysis was found.
- Long-term persistence of SICI reduction into adulthood was not searched.

## Do any studies directly measure static posture (spinal alignment, scapular position, foot posture) in ADHD or DCD children?

### Takeaway
**I found no published study measuring static spinal alignment, scapular position, or foot posture in children with ADHD**, and no ADHD–scoliosis cohort. The only ADHD-specific item is a **trial registration with no results** (NCT07180758: posture rating, Foot Posture Index and sway). The only near-alignment finding is **higher right-ankle passive ROM in ADHD** in one preliminary study. So **no direct evidence links childhood ADHD/DCD to later static posture or body shape.**

### Cited Findings
- ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07180758, "Prediction of ADHD in Children Using Pedobarographic and Postural Data". Baseline measures: **New York Posture Rating Test, Foot Posture Index (FPI-6)**, sway. No results posted (SS). — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07180758)
- Search for an ADHD–scoliosis nationwide cohort returned **nothing ADHD-specific**. Background: global paediatric scoliosis prevalence is about **1.65% (95% CI 1.38–1.94)** (SS). — [PMC12570432](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12570432/)
- Background, not ADHD: slight thoracic hyperkyphosis is common in ordinary children. More than half of 582 Spanish 8–12-year-olds had it; 34.2% of 152 Serbian/Bulgarian children had hyperkyphosis, linked to body composition (SS). This is a base-rate caution for attributing kyphosis to ADHD. — [PMC7177740](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7177740/); [PMC10814759](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10814759/)
- Background: a 2025 study in preadolescents linked flatfoot with scoliosis and impaired coordination, but not with kyphosis or forward head posture; ADHD not assessed (SS). — [Frontiers Pediatr 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pediatrics/articles/10.3389/fped.2025.1621792/xml)
- Right-ankle passive ROM higher in ADHD (p=0.021). This is the nearest ADHD-specific musculoskeletal measure related to foot posture (SS). **[weak]** — [PMC11430528](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11430528/)
- DCD indirect evidence: delayed anticipatory postural adjustments ([Verbecque 2021](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/balance-control-in-individuals-with-developmental-coordination-di/)) and delayed or absent anticipatory trunk muscle activation ([Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40474-018-0130-9)). These are **dynamic control** findings, not static alignment.

### Inferences
- The causal chain "childhood ADHD/DCD motor problems → adult posture or body shape" has **no direct supporting study** at the alignment level. Any report statement should be framed as a hypothesis built from indirect links:
  - poor trunk anticipatory control,
  - possible joint laxity,
  - lower participation in sport and physical activity,
  - possibly more sedentary time. [speculative]
- Because hyperkyphosis and flatfoot are common in ordinary children, an adult's posture cannot be attributed to ADHD without comparison data. Strong alternative explanations include activity history, body composition and habitual sitting. [moderate as a methodological point]

### Gaps
- DCD-specific static posture studies (e.g. foot posture, flat foot, scapular dyskinesis in DCD) were **not searched** because the budget ran out. This is a priority follow-up.
- No longitudinal data link childhood motor-coordination status to adult alignment.

## Overall: evidence for and against the working hypothesis ("normal maximal strength [layers 1–2], impaired control [layers 3–5]")

### Takeaway
The hypothesis is **broadly consistent with the available evidence, but over-specified**.

- **For:**
  - normal maximal pinch strength alongside more variable grip force in the same ADHD children (Hotham 2018)
  - similar muscular fitness in a large cohort while coordination lags (Opper 2022, coordinator-provided)
  - replicated reduced SICI
  - persistent mirror overflow
  - poorer balance under sensory challenge
- **Against or complicating:**
  1. Layer 2 is **unmeasured** in ADHD, not shown to be normal.
  2. DCD, present in roughly 30–55% of ADHD, has some reports of **reduced strength and power**, and a different motor-unit firing pattern.
  3. Joint hypermobility (layer 1) is claimed but **weakly and inconsistently** supported.
  4. Several layer-4 functions are **preserved**: implicit sequence learning, adult force-matching learning, static balance in one study.
  5. Some apparent "control" deficits may reflect **attention-related intra-individual variability** rather than neuromuscular control.
  6. **Medication** alters both SICI and posture.

### Cited Findings
- Normal maximal pinch strength with abnormal grip-force variability in the same off-medication ADHD sample. — [Hotham 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-017-1822-8)
- DCD: "equally able to generate force but show more variability". — [Smits-Engelsman 2008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167945708000171)
- DCD strength reduced in some studies, not others. — [Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40474-018-0130-9)
- Replicated reduced SICI. — [Gilbert 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21321335/); [Gilbert 2019](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007899); [reliability 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40411982/)
- Preserved procedural sequence learning. — [2020 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33192824/)
- Static balance and plantar sensitivity null in one study. — [Res Dev Disabil 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0891422212001382)
- Go RT more variable in ADHD (attention-variability confound). — [Gilbert 2019](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007899)
- Methylphenidate improves posture. — [PMC4854903](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4854903/); MPH improves SICI. — [Psychiatria Danubina 2016](https://www.psychiatria-danubina.com/UserDocsImages/pdf/dnb_vol28_sup1/dnb_vol28_sup1_99.pdf)
- Persistence:
  - teen motor problems still parent-reported ([VU portal](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/motor-coordination-problems-in-children-and-adolescents-with-adhd-2/))
  - overflow not fully resolved in adolescence ([PMC13359276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13359276))
  - beat-synchronization deficit in both children and adults ([Puyjarinet 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11295-w))
  - mixed adult tapping findings ([bioRxiv 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.24.887802v2.full))

### Inferences
- A more defensible version of the hypothesis: **"In ADHD, maximal force capacity shows no evidence of a deficit at the group level. Deficits are concentrated in the precision and consistency of submaximal and anticipatory control, inhibitory motor physiology, and coordination tasks, especially balance under sensory challenge, timing and handwriting quality. These deficits are larger and broader, possibly including strength and power, in the 30–55% with comorbid DCD."** [moderate as a synthesis]
- On the client's causal question: the evidence supports "childhood ADHD-associated motor problems were real and probably persisted in part". It does **not** support "they were a major cause of current posture or body shape", because no study measures alignment outcomes. Plausible indirect pathways should be presented as hypotheses: trunk stability, laxity, and reduced activity or sport participation over the years. [speculative]

### Gaps
- No study links childhood ADHD motor status to adult posture.
- No ADHD layer-2 data.
- No ADHD coactivation or RFD data.
- No ADHD proprioception data.
- No DCD static-posture search was done.
- Interhemispheric inhibition was not searched.
