## Q1. Do motor coordination problems in ADHD/DCD persist into adulthood? (incl. soft signs / mirror movements)

### Takeaway
In a substantial share of people, though not all, childhood motor coordination problems persist into adolescence and adulthood.
- Follow-ups find roughly half of clumsy children still differ from controls 10 years later. Small adult DCD samples are slower and more variable on every motor task tested. [moderate]
- The Gothenburg ADHD+DCD cohort had much worse adult outcomes, but those outcomes were mainly psychiatric and social. I could not retrieve its adult *motor* or *physical* outcomes.
- Data on soft signs and mirror movements in *adult* ADHD are very thin. [weak]

### Cited Findings
- **Gothenburg cohort, age 22.** Rasmussen & Gillberg (2000), "Natural outcome of ADHD with developmental coordination disorder at age 22 years: a controlled, longitudinal, community-based study," *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 39(11):1424–31. 55 of 61 index children were followed from age 7 to 22. **58% of the ADHD/DCD group had a "poor outcome" vs 13% of comparisons.** In this study "poor outcome" is a composite of psychosocial outcomes; the motor-specific results were not retrieved. [moderate] (search summary only) — [GHDx record](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/node/120871); [informath summary](https://www.informath.org/apprise/a6400/b4.htm)
- **Same cohort, median age ~30** (2022 Gothenburg doctoral thesis). Groups: ADHD+DCD n=62, index comparisons n=51, registry-matched n=360. The ADHD+DCD group had significantly more psychiatric diagnoses, psychoactive prescriptions and sick pensions. **"A substantial minority in all groups did not experience any of these four adverse outcomes."** [moderate] — [GUPEA thesis record](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/items/45c2c131-4100-4acc-a83f-4d93f18a238f); [GU project page](https://www.gu.se/en/research/neurodevelopmental-disorders-essence-early-detection-and-outcome-in-adulthood)
- **Cohort baseline.** ADHD+DCD n=62 (11 female), diagnosed at mean age 6.6; population-matched comparisons n=51 (10 female). — [BMJ Open 2022 via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/16c4198172ca4848a1329b7d5426d038)
- **Scientific caveat on this cohort.** The DAMP concept and the Gothenburg data were the subject of a well-known Swedish controversy; Gillberg received a suspended sentence and a fine over the handling of the research data. DAMP research comes almost entirely from this one group. [context, not a finding] — [Wikipedia: Gothenburg Study of Children with DAMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg_Study_of_Children_with_DAMP)
- **Cousins & Smyth (2003), "Developmental coordination impairments in adulthood,"** *Hum Mov Sci* 22:433–459. 19 adults aged 18–65 with a DCD/dyspraxia diagnosis or a self-reported matching history, vs matched controls.
  - The DCD group did worse on *every* task: dexterity, handwriting, balance, ball skills, reaction and movement time, sequencing, dual-task.
  - "Slowness and variability of movement was a pervasive feature."
  - [moderate for "deficits can persist"; weak for prevalence, because the sample is small and self-selected] — [Lancaster record](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/18770)
- **Cantell, Smyth & Ahonen** (Finnish follow-up of motor delay detected at age 5, reassessed at 15). **46% of the early-delay group still differed from controls** on motor and perceptual tasks. Clumsy adolescents rated themselves less physically and scholastically competent, but not lower in social acceptance or self-worth. [moderate] (search summary only) — [BISp record](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU199408073664)
- **Losse et al. (1991), "Clumsiness in children – do they grow out of it? A ten year follow-up study,"** *Dev Med Child Neurol* 33:55–68. The citation was confirmed, but the findings were not retrieved. — [UCL Discovery (citing doc)](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10018816/1/128561_Redacted.pdf)
- **"30–70% persistence."** A secondary review states that daily-functioning difficulties continue into adulthood in 30–70% of people diagnosed with DCD in childhood, citing Cousins & Smyth among others. [weak] (search summary only; the citing review was not identified)
- **Arvo Ylppö Longitudinal Study (Finland).** n=656; motor skills tested at age 5, accelerometry at 25. People at DCD risk at age 5 took fewer total steps at 25: "DCD risk status continues to negatively impact on levels of physical activity into early adulthood." [moderate] — [JYX record](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_81083)
- **Adults with ADHD, objective motor battery.** 25 adults with ADHD vs 23 controls. Motor problems were far more common in ADHD, e.g. **76% vs 37% failed one-leg dynamic balance**. I believe this is Stray et al. 2013, *Behav Brain Funct* 9:18, "Motor regulation problems and pain in adults diagnosed with ADHD", but the title is from memory and was not verified this session. [weak: small sample] (search summary only) — [BBF table 3](https://behavioralandbrainfunctions.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1744-9081-9-18/tables/3)
- **Swedish clinical sample, 403 adults with ADHD.** 32% recalled below-average physical-education performance at age 12, about double the rate in non-clinical samples. Childhood clumsiness was linked to having been bullied. [weak: retrospective self-report] — [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/2022/10/study-of-adults-with-adhd-links-childhood-clumsiness-to-a-higher-likelihood-of-being-bullied-64008)
- **Cardiff, 16 adults self-referred for motor assessment.** Their main concerns were executive functioning and activities of daily living. **Gross-motor skills were among the *least* common self-reported concerns**, so adults may not label their own motor problems as such. [weak] — [ORCA, Purcell, Scott-Roberts & Kirby](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/113921)
- **Soft signs in adult ADHD** (Turkish case-control, *Anadolu Psikiyatri Derg* 2014). The ADHD group did significantly worse on motor coordination and finger–thumb opposition, and also on synkinesis and gaze impersistence. [weak] — [IMR Press](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/AP/15/4/10.5455/apd.155785)
- **Mirror movements, general neurology.** Physiological mirror movements are normal in children up to about age 10. Their persistence or reappearance in adults "is usually regarded as pathologic." [moderate as background] — [Front Neurol 2021 mini-review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8488104/)
- **Mirror overflow in children with ADHD.**
  - Children with ADHD show more mirror overflow than controls, especially when tapping with the non-dominant hand (EEG study, 25 vs 25). [weak-moderate] — [bioRxiv 2021](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.04.23.441107.full.pdf)
  - Controls and girls with ADHD showed steady age-related reduction of overflow, whereas **boys with ADHD showed little improvement through age 14**. This is likely Cole et al. 2008, *Neurology*. [moderate] (search summary only; page attribution uncertain) — [PMC2597066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2597066)

### Inferences
- **Persistence is common but heterogeneous.** Roughly a half-to-majority of children with clear motor coordination problems still show measurable deficits in adolescence or adulthood, and a meaningful minority do not. This fits a "some persist, some catch up" pattern, not a uniform lifelong deficit. [moderate]
- **The ADHD+DCD subgroup seems to have the worst adult trajectory.** But the outcomes documented for that group are psychiatric, social and occupational, not posture or body shape. [moderate]
- **Adult motor deficits are best described as slowness, variability, poor sequencing and dual-task cost** (Cousins & Smyth). Those are motor-*control* features, not static alignment features. A person can have persistent coordination problems without a distinctive standing posture, and the reverse. [speculative link to posture]

### Gaps
- **Adult motor and physical outcomes of the Gothenburg cohort.** Clumsiness, accidents, BMI or pain at ages 16, 22 or 30 could not be retrieved. Hellgren et al. 1993/1994 may report "general health at 16", but only the psychiatric paper's metadata surfaced. The search budget was exhausted before a follow-up search.
- **Losse 1991 findings.** I recall that most of the clumsy group still had motor difficulties at age 16, but this is unverified.
- **Mirror movements in adult ADHD.** No adult ADHD vs control study was found.
- **Proportion of adult ADHD meeting DCD criteria.** No reliable adult prevalence figure was found.

## Q2. Physical outcomes in adults with ADHD/DCD: fitness, obesity/body composition, musculoskeletal and chronic pain, injuries, falls, back pain

### Takeaway
- ADHD is robustly associated with obesity and the association is stronger in adults. [strong for association]
- In children and adolescents, ADHD is robustly associated with unintentional injuries. [strong for association]
- Chronic pain is common in adult ADHD clinical samples, but much of the association runs through mood, anxiety and catastrophising. [moderate]
- Children with DCD have higher BMI and waist circumference and lower fitness. DCD risk predicts lower adult physical activity. [moderate]
- Data on adult DCD pain, adult falls, sports injuries and back pain specifically are sparse. [weak/gap]

### Cited Findings
- **Obesity.** Cortese et al. (2016), "Association between ADHD and obesity: a systematic review and meta-analysis," *Am J Psychiatry* 173(1):34–43. 42 studies, 728,136 individuals (48,161 with ADHD). The association was significant in both children and adults: **adults OR ≈1.55 (1.32–1.81) vs children OR ≈1.20 (1.05–1.37)**; the age-split ORs come from a secondary citation. It held after controlling for study setting, country and quality. [strong for association; causality not established] — [AJP](https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/abs/10.1176/appi.ajp.2015.15101315); [Update 2016 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5247534/)
- **Unintentional injuries (children and adolescents).** Ruiz-Goikoetxea et al. (2018), *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*.
  - **Pooled OR 1.53 (1.40–1.67)** from 28 studies (350,938 people with ADHD). **Pooled HR 1.39 (1.06–1.83)** from 4 studies.
  - In self-controlled designs, ADHD medication was protective: **0.879 (0.838–0.922)**.
  - Fracture-specific results were not retrieved.
  - [strong for association in youth] — [Southampton ePrints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/417430)
- **Chronic pain in women with childhood ADHD/ASD.** Asztély et al. (2019), *J Pain Res* 12:2925–2932. 77 of 100 Swedish women followed for 16–19 years.
  - **76.6% reported chronic pain.**
  - Chronic widespread pain **32.5%** and chronic regional pain **44.2%**, vs 11.9% and 23.9% in a Swedish population survey.
  - Women on ongoing stimulant treatment had **lower** chronic widespread pain.
  - Limitations: small sample, **no healthy controls**, women only.
  - [moderate-weak] — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/3e0241e942c74348ae7b904b9a4db479); [MDedge summary](https://mdedge.com/content/chronic-pain-more-common-women-adhd-or-asd)
- **General population, England** (Stickley et al.; n=7,403). ADHD symptoms predicted extreme pain interference, but **the effect shrank after adjusting for common mental disorders**. [moderate] (search summary only, via a 2025 paper) — [Sci Rep 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-025-95864-4)
- **Japanese internet survey** (n=4,028, aged 20–64). The chronic-pain group (n=1,465) scored higher on all ASRS ADHD-symptom variables. [weak-moderate: cross-sectional, self-report] — [Sci Rep 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41598-025-95864-4)
- **Japanese pain-centre study** (n=958).
  - ADHD traits were about 2.4× more common than in the general population.
  - ADHD symptoms (not ASD symptoms) were associated with higher pain intensity and extremely severe chronic pain.
  - **The association was mediated by anxiety/depression and catastrophising.**
  - [weak-moderate] — [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/adhd-traits-chronic-pain-30584/); [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/body-pain-catastrophizing-adhd-asd/)
- **Kasahara et al. 2025 mini-review** (*Front Pharmacol*). It cites a **72.5% comorbid ADHD rate in chronic-pain patients** (from a 2020 Kasahara clinic sample) and frames ADHD as a contributor to *nociplastic* pain, with comorbidity with fibromyalgia and chronic low back pain. [weak for prevalence: specialised clinic sample] — [Front Pharmacol](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1500313/full)
- **2026 scoping review on ADHD and chronic pain:** 50 studies, mostly observational. — [Front Psychiatry 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1837517/full)
- **Fatigue in adult DCD.** Thomas & Christopher (2017/18): 53 adults with DCD, 84 with CFS, 52 typically developing. The DCD group was worse than typical adults on all measures including fatigue, but better than CFS. [weak-moderate] — [Bath Spa record](https://researchspace.bathspa.ac.uk/10544/)
- **DCD and body composition in children.** Cairney et al., *CMAJ* 2010;182(11):1167–72. Children with probable DCD had higher BMI and waist circumference in grade 4, and the difference persisted over 2 years. [moderate] (secondary summary) — [summary](https://yourtherapysource.com/?p=831); [McMaster experts](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/911157)
  - Joshi et al. 2015 (*Hum Mov Sci*) followed BMI and waist over ~5 years in probable DCD; details were not retrieved.
- **DCD and cardiorespiratory fitness trajectories:** BJSM paper by the Cairney group; only the title and metadata surfaced. — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/45/15/1196.info)
- **Low motor competence and bone (Raine cohort).** Motor competence measured at ages 10, 14 and 17, bone density at 20 (n≈1,043). Low motor competence was associated with **reduced peak bone mass in young adults**. [moderate] (search summary only) — [JYX record](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/86961)
- **Hypermobility + DCD + pain in adults** (Bournemouth: Clark, Carr, Breen). This is only a *hypothesis*: "adults with JHS show poor movement patterns which contribute to biomechanical dysfunction and continuing pain." The authors note "there are currently no tools to assess DCD in adults with musculoskeletal pain" and developed the FDQ-9. [speculative/weak: conference abstract] — [Bournemouth abstract](https://staffprofiles.bournemouth.ac.uk/display/conference/17052); [FDQ validation PDF](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/20518/1/Validation_of_the_FDQ%20v3.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Shared factors could explain part of the body-composition outcome.** The adult obesity association (OR ~1.5) and lower adult physical activity in people at DCD risk plausibly contribute to adult body shape in some people with childhood ADHD/DCD. But the meta-analytic associations are population averages and are confounded by medication, sleep, eating behaviour and socioeconomic status. [moderate for association; speculative for any individual]
- **The ADHD–pain link looks largely central and psychological, not structural.** Mediation by mood and catastrophising, the nociplastic framing, and lower pain with stimulant treatment all point that way, rather than to posture or alignment. This argues *against* "posture → pain" as the main ADHD pain pathway. [moderate]

### Gaps
- No adult DCD cohort with measured fitness, body composition **and** pain was found.
- No data were found on falls in adult ADHD/DCD.
- No data were found on adult sports injuries in ADHD/DCD.
- No data were found specifically on low back pain incidence in adult ADHD versus controls, beyond the review statement on comorbidity.
- Cortese 2016 adult-only pooled OR needs checking in the full text.

## Q3. Joint hypermobility (GJH, hEDS/HSD) in ADHD/autism, and how hypermobility relates to posture, proprioception, pain and muscle control

### Takeaway
- In **clinical adult** ADHD and autism samples, GJH is clearly over-represented: ADHD vs controls aOR ≈4.7 by Beighton score, ≈6.9 for *symptomatic* GJH. [moderate]
- **The association is absent in a non-clinical adult population and in 11-year-old schoolchildren.** So it is not universal and may be limited to clinical or symptomatic cases, or appear only after puberty. [moderate counter-evidence]
- Hypermobility is prospectively linked to adolescent *peripheral* joint pain, but **not to spinal pain**. [moderate-strong]
- Hypermobility is linked to poorer lower-limb joint position sense and to more postural sway. [moderate-weak]
- Evidence that hypermobility produces a particular *static posture* is mostly clinical opinion. [speculative/weak]

### Cited Findings
- **Glans et al. (2021), *European Psychiatry*, case-control.** 431 adults with ADHD vs 417 controls; GJH measured by Beighton score, physical exam and the 5PQ questionnaire.
  - **aOR 4.65 (3.01–7.18) by Beighton**; **aOR 1.86 (1.39–2.48) by 5PQ**.
  - **Symptomatic GJH** (GJH plus pain and/or skin signs): **aOR 6.94 (4.05–11.89)** by Beighton, 2.66 by 5PQ.
  - Group-level percentages were not retrieved.
  - [moderate] — [PMC9471100](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9471100/); [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/association-between-adult-adhd-and-generalised-joint-hypermobility-with-and-without-systemic-manifestations-a-casecontrol-study/C5D362A3EC1CCB0F953DDB83C6204C23); [PubMed 34560594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34560594/)
- **Csecs et al. (2022), "Joint Hypermobility Links Neurodivergence to Dysautonomia and Pain,"** *Front Psychiatry* 12:786916. 109 UK adults with formal neurodevelopmental diagnoses.
  - Hypermobility in **51%** of the neurodivergent group vs a **general-population rate of 20%**. The comparison is to a literature estimate, not to measured controls.
  - A separate comparison group of n=57 was used for orthostatic intolerance and musculoskeletal symptoms.
  - The neurodivergent group reported more orthostatic intolerance and musculoskeletal pain.
  - **The number of hypermobile joints statistically mediated** the neurodivergence → dysautonomia/pain relationship.
  - [moderate-weak: modest sample, cross-sectional mediation, no matched controls for prevalence] — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.786916/full); [PubMed 35185636](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35185636/)
- **Glans, Bejerot & Humble (2017), *BJPsych Open* 3(5):236–242, non-clinical population.** 1,039 Swedish adults surveyed, 887 analysed. **"We found no associations between GJH and sub-syndromal symptoms of ADHD, ASD or DCD"**; DCD was screened with a clumsiness question. The authors conclude the GJH–NDD link "is presumably limited to clinical populations." [moderate counter-evidence] — [PubMed 28959454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28959454/); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/generalised-joint-hypermobility-and-neurodevelopmental-traits-ina-nonclinical-adult-population/EC50D7D335AFDC5A77F6DDBF0FE3B739)
- **Glans et al. (2025), *BJPsych Open*, 11-year-old schoolchildren.** 223 of 348 eligible fourth-graders took part; Beighton score measured in 207; median Beighton 1.
  - **No association between joint hypermobility, musculoskeletal pain and neurodevelopmental problems.**
  - The authors call for follow-up through puberty. The 64% participation rate raises possible selection bias.
  - [moderate-weak counter-evidence] — [PMC12641418](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12641418/); [GU press release](https://www.gu.se/en/news/study-finds-no-link-between-joint-hypermobility-pain-and-neurodevelopmental-disorders-in-children-0)
- **Autism, adults (Swedish case-control, 2022):** ASD–GJH aOR 3.1 (1.9–5.2). [moderate] (search summary only) — [Front Psychiatry 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.803334/full)
- **Child ADHD study cited in that paper:** generalised hypermobility in 32% of 54 ADHD patients vs 14% of comparisons. [weak] (search summary only)
- **Baeza-Velasco et al. (2025), *Autism*, meta-analysis.** 20 studies; most found a significant association. Pooled prevalence of joint hypermobility and related connective-tissue disorders in autistic people was **22.3%–39%**, above general-population estimates. No equivalent ADHD meta-analysis was found. [moderate] — [CiteDrive record, DOI 10.1177/13623613251328059](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/autism-in-the-context-of-joint-hypermobility-hypermobility-spectrum-disorders-and-ehlersdanlos-syndromes-a-systematic-review-and-prevalence-meta-analyses)
- **ALSPAC prospective cohort** (Tobias et al. 2013, *Arthritis Rheum*). Beighton score at age 13.8 (hypermobility = ≥6 of 9; 4.6% hypermobile); pain measured at 17.8; n=2,901.
  - Increased risk of **shoulder pain (aOR ≈1.7), knee pain (≈1.8) and ankle/foot pain (≈1.8)**.
  - **No equivalent association at the spine, elbows, hands or hips.**
  - Knee-pain OR was 1.6 in non-obese vs **11.0 in obese** participants, consistent with a mechanical-load pathway.
  - [moderate-strong] — [Bristol research record](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/hypermobility-is-a-risk-factor-for-musculoskeletal-pain-in-adoles/); [Bristol news](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2013/9169.html)
- **Proprioception in hypermobility.** Smith et al. (2013), *Rheumatol Int* 33(11):2709–16, systematic review and meta-analysis. 5 studies, 254 people.
  - **Poorer lower-limb joint position sense (p<0.001) and threshold to detection of movement (p<0.001).**
  - Upper-limb evidence was unclear.
  - [moderate-weak: few small studies] — [UEA ePrints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/46950/)
- **Standing tolerance in joint hypermobility syndrome** (*BMC Musculoskelet Disord* 2021). People with symptomatic JHS stood about 7 minutes on average and none completed the 15-minute protocol. People with GJH but no pain, and controls, all completed it. Fidgeting did not differ between groups. Anteroposterior sway differed during quiet standing, possibly reflecting ankle muscle weakness. [weak: small] — [PMC8638551](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8638551/)
- **14-year-old girls with GJH** (at least one hypermobile knee). They showed larger postural sway with eyes closed and altered knee muscle co-activation. [weak] — [KU record](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/da/publications/girls-with-generalized-joint-hypermobility-display-changed-muscle)
- **Children with GJH told to "straighten your back."** The cue increased sacral slope and decreased thoracic kyphosis; lumbar lordosis did not change significantly. The authors advise **against** using this cue to reach optimal posture in this group. [weak] — [PMC5237459](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5237459/)
- **Clinical handout (Russek).** Common deviations seen in HSD/EDS are sway-back, kypho-lordotic and forward head, but "there is very little research evidence on posture and pain in this population." [speculative: expert clinical opinion] — [Russek HSD posture PDF](https://webspace.clarkson.edu/%7Elrussek/docs/hypermobility/RussekHSDPosture.pdf)
- **DCD and joint hypermobility syndrome (JHS)** share functional difficulties in children, with "limited understanding of the relationship." — [Bournemouth abstract](https://staffprofiles.bournemouth.ac.uk/display/conference/17052)

### Inferences
- **Hypermobility is a credible alternative or co-explanation for some adult ADHD body complaints** (peripheral joint pain, standing intolerance, sway, poor lower-limb position sense). In clinical adult ADHD samples it is several times more common. [moderate]
- **But hypermobility is not a reliable marker of ADHD in the general population or in childhood** (Glans 2017, 2025). It cannot be assumed in an individual and must be measured. [moderate]
- **ALSPAC found no spinal pain association.** That weakens any claim that hypermobility → "bad spinal posture" → back pain. [moderate]
- **A plausible mechanism, not demonstrated:** lax passive restraints + reduced joint position sense + sway → the person "hangs" on ligaments (sway-back, knee hyperextension). There is no direct study linking GJH to measured sagittal alignment in adults. [speculative]

### Gaps
- No ADHD-specific hypermobility meta-analysis was found.
- No study measured sagittal alignment (lordosis, kyphosis, sway) in adults with GJH vs controls.
- No study tested whether hypermobility mediates a childhood DCD → adult posture link.
- Beighton cut-offs by age and sex (e.g., the 2017 international criteria) were not verified this session.

## Q4. Proprioception and body awareness (interoception, body schema) in adults with ADHD/DCD

### Takeaway
- Direct adult data are scarce. Two small heartbeat-perception (interoception) studies in adult ADHD contradict each other. [weak]
- Joint position sense deficits are documented in **children** with DCD. [moderate-weak]
- Joint position sense deficits are also documented in **hypermobility** (any age). [moderate-weak]
- No adult ADHD joint position sense study was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Kutscheidt et al. (2019),** *ADHD Atten Def Hyp Disord*. 14 adults with ADHD vs 16 controls. ADHD adults did **significantly worse** on heartbeat counting. [weak] — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12402-019-00299-3)
- **PLoS ONE (2018).** 24 adults with ADHD vs 23 controls. Performance on heartbeat perception and the interoception questionnaire was **"strikingly similar"** across groups. [weak] — [PMC6185835](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6185835/)
- **Children with DCD** (*J Rehabil Med* 2020). Impaired joint position sense at knee and ankle; balance was worse as joint position sense got worse. [moderate-weak] — [JRM PDF](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/jrm/article/download/3830/5889)
- **Children with ADHD** (*Front Hum Neurosci* 2025). 25% and 34% lower hip joint torques in dynamic and static balance tasks. The authors infer "impaired proprioception and neuromuscular control" and implicate the cerebellum. [weak: indirect inference from torque; children] — [Front Hum Neurosci](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1630049/pdf)
- **Adults with autism** (preprint, 16 vs 16). **Intact** proprioceptive acuity but more sway during sensory adaptation, interpreted as a sensory-*integration* problem, not a problem with joint position sense itself. [weak: preprint] — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/644617v1.full)
- **Hypermobility.** Poorer lower-limb joint position sense and movement detection (Smith 2013; see Q3). — [UEA ePrints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/46950/)
- **Adolescents.** A study of knee joint position sense and kinematic control in relation to motor competency in 13–14-year-olds exists; its findings were not retrieved. — [PMC11440888](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11440888/)

### Inferences
- **Sensory-integration problems may matter more than raw acuity.** If adults with childhood DCD have proprioceptive problems, these may lie more in integrating senses during movement than in raw position-sense acuity (autism analogy; Cousins & Smyth's slowness and variability). [speculative]
- **Hypermobility is a measurable confounder** for any proprioception deficit an individual has. [moderate]

### Gaps
- No adult ADHD or adult DCD joint position sense study was found.
- No body-schema or body-image study in adult DCD was retrieved.
- Interoception tasks based on heartbeat counting have known validity problems. This is from prior knowledge and not verified this session, so neither adult ADHD result is decisive.

## Q5. Developmental chain: childhood motor competence → adolescent/adult physical activity → body composition and fitness

### Takeaway
- A widely used conceptual model (Stodden 2008; reviewed by Robinson 2015) proposes a spiral: motor competence ↔ physical activity ↔ fitness ↔ weight status, strengthening with age. Supporting evidence exists but is still described as limited. [moderate]
- DCD-specific longitudinal data support "lower activity in adulthood" and "higher BMI and waist in childhood", but stop short of adult body composition. [moderate]

### Cited Findings
- **Robinson et al. (2015),** "Motor competence and its effect on positive developmental trajectories of health," *Sports Med* 45:1273–1284. Reviews the Stodden 2008 model of synergistic links between motor competence, perceived motor competence, physical activity, fitness and obesity. Notes "limited evidence was available to support or refute the model hypotheses" when the model was proposed. [moderate] — [SpringerMedicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/motor-competence-and-its-effect-on-positive-developmental-trajec/22254304)
- **Barnett et al. 2021 systematic review** of longitudinal and experimental evidence for the model: 585 records screened, 43 included (conference abstract). Detailed conclusions were not retrieved. A 2021 *Sports Med* paper still describes the evidence base as limited. — [record](https://oasisbr.ibict.br/vufind/Record/RCAP_4244a8b32c1f0ff5e9cabd70f16fab44); [Sports Med 2021](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40279-021-01516-8)
- **Arvo Ylppö (Finland).** DCD risk at age 5 → fewer steps at age 25 (n=656). [moderate] — [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_81083)
- **Cairney, *CMAJ* 2010.** Probable DCD → higher BMI and waist in grade 4, persisting over 2 years. [moderate] — [summary](https://yourtherapysource.com/?p=831)
- **CATCH cohort (design paper, *BMC Public Health* 2015).** Planned 600 children aged 4–5 (300 probable DCD) tested yearly for 4 years on DCD → activity → fitness → body composition pathways. [design only] — [PMC4687347](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4687347/)
- **Preschoolers** (*Front Pediatr* 2022). Aerobic fitness and moderate-to-vigorous activity were negatively associated with adiposity after adjusting for MABC-2 motor score. Whether fitness protects children with DCD from obesity "requires longitudinal investigation." — [Front Pediatr XML](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/pediatrics/articles/10.3389/fped.2022.756862/xml)
- **Measurement caveat.** Standard fitness tests may be confounded by coordination in adolescents with DCD: "poor coordination and inconsistent motor performance may confound the outcome." — [Hands et al. 2013 abstract](https://researchonline.nd.edu.au/health_conference/38)
- **Raine cohort.**
  - Low motor competence at ages 10–17 → reduced peak bone mass at 20. — [JYX](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/86961)
  - In another Raine analysis (n=666), at age 14 normal-weight youth scored better on the McCarron motor assessment (MAND) than obese youth. [moderate] (search summary only; exact paper unverified) — [Hands bepress](https://works.bepress.com/beth-hands/17/)
- **Finnish adolescents.** Girls with low motor competence may get insufficient skeletal loading (latent profile analysis, 4-year follow-up). [weak: conference abstract] — [PMC10493983](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10493983/)
- **Raine: BMI trajectory → posture.** Childhood BMI trajectory classes (Very High, High, Ascending) were strongly associated with **Hyperlordotic or Sway** posture at age 14 (n=1,373). [moderate] — [Curtin espace](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/6760)

### Inferences
- **There is a plausible *indirect* chain from childhood motor problems to adolescent body shape:** low motor competence → less activity and loading → higher BMI and lower fitness and bone → more sway or hyperlordotic standing posture.
  - Each link has some evidence: Ylppö, Cairney, Raine bone, and Smith 2011 BMI → posture.
  - The *whole chain* has never been tested in one cohort, and each link is a modest population-level association.
  - [moderate for individual links; speculative for the full chain]
- **This chain runs through modifiable variables** (activity, fitness, BMI), not through a fixed "developmental posture defect". That is useful for the rehabilitation framing. [inference]

### Gaps
- No study was found that follows DCD/ADHD children to adult posture measurement.
- The Barnett review conclusions and effect sizes were not retrieved.

## Q6. Posture science: do static postural deviations predict pain or dysfunction? What determines adult posture? Any childhood motor coordination → adolescent posture link?

### Takeaway
- **Static posture is a weak and inconsistent predictor of spinal pain.** Systematic reviews find no established link between sagittal spinal curves and pain, and "no consensus on causality." [strong for "no established causal link"]
- **Small associations exist in some adolescent data:** non-neutral standing posture and back pain, sway-back boys. [weak-moderate]
- **Some prospective data run the opposite way:** in Raine females, slumped or forward-head sitting at 17 predicted *less* persistent neck pain. [moderate]
- **Scapular dyskinesis** modestly predicts shoulder pain in athletes: RR 1.43, but 65% with dyskinesis stay pain-free. [moderate]
- **Adult posture is substantially heritable and structurally constrained:** lordosis h²≈59%, kyphosis ≈61%, pelvic incidence fixed after skeletal maturity. It also tracks BMI, sex, mood and exercise. [moderate]
- **No study links childhood motor coordination or DCD to adolescent posture types.** [gap]

### Cited Findings
- **Swain et al. (2020),** "No consensus on causality of spine postures or physical exposure and low back pain: a systematic review of systematic reviews," *J Biomech* 102:109312.
  - 4,285 records screened, 41 reviews included.
  - "Both positive and null associations" were reported for posture, prolonged sitting and standing, bending and twisting, awkward postures, whole-body vibration and heavy work.
  - Meta-analyses leaned more toward association; **reviews limited to prospective studies were least consistent.**
  - [strong] — [Monash record](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/no-consensus-on-causality-of-spine-postures-or-physical-exposure-/); [ACU record](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8wq8w/no-consensus-on-causality-of-spine-postures-or-physical-exposure-and-low-back-pain-a-systematic-review-of-systematic-reviews)
- **Christensen & Hartvigsen (2008),** systematic critical review of 54 epidemiological studies, quality rated with STROBE. The evidence **"does not support an association between sagittal spinal curves and health including spinal pain."** I believe the journal is *J Manipulative Physiol Ther* 31(9):690–714 (not verified this session). [moderate-strong] — [PainScience summary](https://www.painscience.com/biblio/no-association-between-measurements-of-spinal-curves-and-pain-review.html)
- **Lederman (2011),** "The fall of the postural-structural-biomechanical model in manual and physical therapies: exemplified by lower back pain," *J Bodyw Mov Ther* debate.
  - Argues there is "no reliably proven relationship" between low back pain and posture, structure or biomechanics.
  - Argues these factors are "unlikely to change in the long-term by manual techniques or even exercise, unless rigorously maintained."
  - McGill, Lee, Fryer and others published invited rebuttals. [expert opinion; weak-moderate] — [PainScience](https://www.painscience.com/biblio/fall-of-the-postural-structural-biomechanical-model.html); [Westminster debate record](https://westminsterresearch.westminster.ac.uk/item/8zvw3/is-a-postural-structural-biomechanical-model-within-manual-therapies-viable-a-jbmt-debate); [McGill response PDF](https://www.backfitpro.com/medical-scientific-articles/2011/[69]McGill,S.M.(2011)Is-a-postural-structural-biomechanical-model-within-manual-therapies-viable(Invited-Response)[J.Bodywork-and-Movement-Ther.].pdf)
- **Slater, Korakakis, O'Sullivan et al. (2019),** "'Sit up straight': time to re-evaluate," *JOSPT* 49(8):562–564.
  - "No strong evidence that one optimal posture exists or that avoiding 'incorrect' postures will prevent low back pain."
  - Natural variation in spinal curves; no single curvature strongly associated with pain.
  - Posture can reflect beliefs and mood; changing position helps.
  - In a survey, ~94% of 544 physiotherapists still rated teaching optimal posture as important.
  - [moderate: expert synthesis] — [UL record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/sit-up-straight-time-to-re-evaluate/)
- **Raine standing posture and back pain** (Smith, O'Sullivan & Straker 2008, *Spine* 33(19):2101–07; 766 adolescents). Non-neutral postures had higher odds for **all** back-pain measures, but **only 7 of 15 analyses were significant**. [weak-moderate: cross-sectional] — [Curtin espace](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/26089)
- **Flemish adolescents before peak height velocity** (Dolphens et al.).
  - Sway-back boys had ~2–3× higher odds of low back or neck pain vs neutral boys.
  - In 557 girls before peak height velocity, **no** posture-cluster associations with spinal pain were significant.
  - [weak-moderate] (search summary only; the exact PMC page for each claim is uncertain) — [PMC3897822](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3897822); [PMC3940786](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3940786)
- **Raine posture subgroups at 14** (Smith 2011; n=1,373). Neutral 29%, Flat 22%, Sway 27%, Hyperlordotic 22%. Higher or rising childhood BMI → more Hyperlordotic and Sway. Normal variation is wide: about 70% are "non-neutral". [moderate] — [Curtin espace](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/6760)
- **Generation XXI (Portugal, age 7).**
  - Girls: sway 26.9%, flat 20.9%, neutral-to-hyperlordotic 52.1%.
  - Boys: sway-to-neutral 58.8%, flat 36.3%, hyperlordotic 4.9%.
  - Higher BMI was associated with sway in girls and with hyperlordotic posture in boys.
  - [moderate] — [U.Porto repository](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/111591)
- **Raine sitting neck posture at 17** (Richards et al. 2016, *Phys Ther* 96(10):1576–87; n=1,108). Four clusters: upright, intermediate, slumped thorax/forward head, erect thorax/forward head.
  - Clusters were associated with **sex, weight and height**.
  - Slumped/forward head → higher odds of **depression**; upright → exercised more.
  - **No difference in odds of neck pain or headache** across clusters.
  - [moderate: cross-sectional] — [Curtin espace](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/29208); [Curtin media release](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/media-release/posture-not-pain-neck-previously-thought/)
- **Raine prospective, ages 17 → 22** (Richards et al. 2021, *Phys Ther*; n=686). Among females, slumped thorax/forward head or intermediate sitting posture was associated with **lower** risk of persistent neck pain than upright posture. [moderate] — [PubMed 33444448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33444448/)
- **Thoracic kyphosis and shoulder pain** (Barrett et al. 2016, *Man Ther*; 10 studies).
  - **Moderate evidence of no difference** in kyphosis between people with and without shoulder pain.
  - **Strong evidence** that maximum shoulder range of motion is greater in erect than slouched postures (an acute mechanical effect).
  - [moderate] — [Herts repository](https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/5872/)
  - A lower-quality review claims increased thoracic curvature contributes to impingement, but acknowledges methodological limits. [weak] — [JPSR](https://jpsr.mums.ac.ir/article_26424.html?lang=en)
- **Scapular dyskinesis** (Hickey et al. 2018, *BJSM* 52(2):102; 5 prospective studies, 419 asymptomatic athletes).
  - **RR 1.43 (1.05–1.93)** for future shoulder pain over 9–24 months: 35% vs 25%.
  - A letter (Littlewood & Cools) notes **65% with dyskinesis never developed pain**.
  - [moderate; athletes only] — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/2/102)
- **Forward head posture and neck pain.** A secondary summary of Mahmoud et al. 2019 (meta-analysis, 15 studies) reports mixed results, with only 5 of 13 studies showing a significant association. [weak] (secondary blog only) — [1HP blog](https://1-hp.org/blog/hpforgamers/the-great-forward-head-posture-debate/)
- **Posture is modifiable by exercise; pain may improve.** Sheikhhoseini et al. 2018 meta-analysis: 7 RCTs, 627 participants. Strong evidence that exercise changes craniovertebral angle; moderate evidence for pain improvement. [moderate] — [UL record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/effectiveness-of-therapeutic-exercise-on-forward-head-posture-a-s/)
- **Genetics (TwinsUK, women only;** 110 monozygotic + 136 dizygotic twin pairs).
  - Heritability: **lumbar lordosis ~59% (42–71%), thoracic kyphosis ~61% (46–74%), pelvic incidence ~41% (19–59%), cervical lordosis ~43% (23–59%)**.
  - The best model had additive genetic + unique environment, with no shared-environment term.
  - Kyphosis was linked to bone mineral density; lordosis to disc degeneration features.
  - [moderate] (search summary only) — [Eur Spine J](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00586-014-3477-6); [Bone & Joint abstract](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/1358-992X.97BSUPP_2.SBPR2014-030)
- **Pelvic incidence (bone morphology).**
  - Becomes fixed at skeletal maturity and strongly predicts lumbar lordosis (via sacral slope).
  - Rises during growth, from about 40° to 46°.
  - The "constant" assumption has been questioned after surgery.
  - [moderate] — [IJSS review PDF](https://www.ijssurgery.com/content/ijss/14/6/949.full.pdf); [SpringerMedicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/is-pelvic-incidence-a-constant-as-everyone-knows-changes-of-pelv/21229876)
- **Childhood motor coordination → posture type.** No Raine (or other) paper was found linking MAND motor scores to posture subgroups, even though Raine collected both. [gap] — [search record: Raine motor-competence papers](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/9aa1497c-628a-4ecf-b3fc-8308209479f0)

### Inferences
- **Determinants of adult static posture, ranked roughly by evidence:**
  1. Genetics and bone morphology: twin heritability ~40–60%; pelvic incidence fixed after growth. [moderate]
  2. Body mass and its childhood trajectory. [moderate]
  3. Sex and height. [moderate]
  4. Mood and beliefs: depression is associated with slumped sitting; the direction is unknown. [weak-moderate]
  5. Exercise habits and muscle endurance. [weak-moderate]
  6. Hypermobility: clinical opinion plus sway data. [weak]
  7. Childhood motor coordination: no direct data. [speculative]
- **For pain, posture is a minor, inconsistent factor.** Psychosocial factors, activity, BMI and central sensitisation have stronger evidence (see Q2). This is a key counterweight to the user's premise: even if childhood motor problems shaped their posture, the posture itself may matter less for pain and function than assumed. [moderate]
- **But posture is not irrelevant to function.** Slouched posture acutely reduces shoulder range of motion, and exercise can modify forward head posture. So posture-*capacity* (being able to adopt and vary positions) may matter more than a habitual resting alignment. [moderate]

### Gaps
- Smith 2008 subgroup-specific odds ratios (flat, sway, hyperlordotic) were not retrieved.
- Mahmoud 2019 primary results were not verified. From prior knowledge (unverified), it reported larger forward head posture in adults with neck pain than in adults without, but not in adolescents.
- No search was done for flat feet and pain, or for anterior pelvic tilt and low back pain specifically (budget exhausted).
- No longitudinal data link childhood DCD or ADHD to adult sagittal alignment.
- The results of the meta-analysis on exercise effects on kyphosis and lordosis angle were not retrieved. — [PMC6488071](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6488071/)

## Q7. Does ADHD itself (inattention, fidgeting, sitting behaviour) affect habitual posture?

### Takeaway
No study was found that measures habitual sitting or standing alignment in ADHD vs controls. Available data concern movement *quantity* and postural *control* (sway), not static alignment. A modest postural-control deficit is reported. [weak]

### Cited Findings
- **Postural control vs seated posture.** One article states that "a few recent studies found that individuals with ADHD have a relatively minor but significant degree of impaired postural control when standing," and that this "has not been assessed in children with ADHD while seated." [weak] (search summary only; source page uncertain)
- **Seated movement and performance** (small study, boys aged 8–12, swivel chair). More movement and spinning correlated with *better* working-memory performance in ADHD and worse in typically developing boys. The author called it one small study. [weak] — [NPR coverage](https://www.npr.org/sections/ed/2015/05/14/404959284/%20fidgeting-)
- **Fidgeting in hypermobility.** Fidgeting during prolonged standing did not differ between symptomatic hypermobility, asymptomatic GJH and controls; sway did differ. [weak] — [PMC8638551](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8638551/)
- **Children with ADHD: balance.** Lower hip torques during balance tasks (see Q4). [weak] — [Front Hum Neurosci 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1630049/pdf)
- **Sustained static posture is not inherently harmful.** "Sitting down for more than 30 mins in one position is NOT dangerous, though moving and changing positions can be helpful" (expert position). — [Slater 2019 record](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/sit-up-straight-time-to-re-evaluate/)

### Inferences
- **ADHD may change posture *variability*, not a fixed shape.** ADHD seems more likely to show up as more position changes and fidgeting, and somewhat poorer standing balance, than as a fixed alignment. The posture-science literature does not regard frequent position change as harmful. [speculative]
- **Indirect routes are better supported than a direct behavioural one.** ADHD → obesity (Cortese) → sway or hyperlordotic posture (Smith 2011). ADHD (clinical) → GJH → sway and standing intolerance. Both are population-level associations. [speculative chain built on moderate links]
- **Mood is a confounder.** Depression is associated with slumped sitting (Richards 2016) and is common in adult ADHD, so an individual's slumped habit could reflect mood or fatigue as much as motor history. [speculative]

### Gaps
- No study measured sitting or standing alignment (photogrammetry, inclinometry) in ADHD children or adults; the photogrammetry search returned no ADHD studies.
- No study tested whether stimulant medication changes posture or sway. Stimulants are associated with fewer injuries and less widespread pain, but posture itself is unstudied.

## Q8. How can an individual reason about whether their childhood condition caused their current body shape? Limits of individual causal attribution, alternative causes, and discriminating tests

### Takeaway
- Population associations (ADHD → obesity, clinical ADHD → hypermobility, DCD → lower activity) **cannot establish that childhood ADHD/DCD caused one specific adult's posture or body shape**. Group-level effects often do not transfer to individuals. [strong methodological point]
- For this user, "childhood motor problems were a *major* cause" is **plausible as one contributor but not demonstrable**. Strong competing determinants exist: genetics and bone morphology, BMI history, hypermobility, mood, activity history.
- Current tests can at least separate some pathways: hypermobility, current motor coordination, fixed vs correctable alignment, and fitness or endurance.

### Cited Findings
- **Group-to-individual generalisability.** Fisher, Medaglia & Jeronimus (2018), "Lack of group-to-individual generalizability is a threat to human subjects research," *PNAS* 115(27):E6106–E6115. Within-person variance was up to ~4× larger than between-person variance in the datasets examined. Group findings should not be assumed to apply to individuals without within-person evidence. [strong as methodology] — [PMC6142277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6142277)
- **Heterogeneity of outcomes.** Even in the Gothenburg ADHD+DCD group, a substantial minority had none of the four adverse adult outcomes. — [GUPEA](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/items/45c2c131-4100-4acc-a83f-4d93f18a238f)
- **Strong non-developmental determinants of curvature.** Twin heritability of ~59–61% for lordosis and kyphosis; pelvic incidence fixed after skeletal maturity. — [Eur Spine J](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00586-014-3477-6); [IJSS](https://www.ijssurgery.com/content/ijss/14/6/949.full.pdf)
- **Hypermobility must be measured, not assumed.** The ADHD–GJH association holds in clinical adults but not in a non-clinical adult population or in 11-year-old children. — [Glans 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9471100/); [Glans 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28959454/); [Glans 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12641418/)
- **Tests used in this literature:**
  - **Beighton score** (9 points; ALSPAC used ≥6 at age ~14). — [Bristol](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/hypermobility-is-a-risk-factor-for-musculoskeletal-pain-in-adoles/)
  - **5PQ** self-report hypermobility questionnaire (used in Glans 2017/2021). — [Glans 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9471100/)
  - **"Symptomatic GJH"**: GJH plus pain and/or skin signs; the most ADHD-specific signal. — [Glans 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9471100/)
- **Adult DCD Checklist (ADC).** Kirby, Edwards, Sugden & Rosenblum (2010), *Res Dev Disabil* 31(1):131–139.
  - First adult DCD screening tool, standardised in the UK and Israel and based on DSM-IV Criterion B.
  - Self-report; covers childhood **and** current difficulties; described for ages ~12+.
  - A secondary source says the paper gives no pass/fail cut-offs (unverified).
  - [moderate as a screening tool, not a diagnosis] — [USW record](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/en/publications/the-development-and-standardization-of-the-adult-developmental-co/); [ADC form (Haifa)](https://chap.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADC_NewEnglish.pdf)
- **Scoping review on assessing DCD in adulthood:** 36 articles, 2008–2024. — [Armstrong scoping review PDF](https://www.dyspraxia.ie/userfiles/files/Assessment%20of%20Developmental%20Coordination%20Disorder%20in%20Adulthood%20-%20A%20Scoping%20Review%20-%20Dr%20Dorothy%20Armstrong.pdf)
- **FDQ-9** (Functional Difficulties Questionnaire), developed for DCD-type difficulties in adults with musculoskeletal pain. — [Bournemouth FDQ PDF](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/20518/1/Validation_of_the_FDQ%20v3.pdf)
- **Objective adult motor batteries** have been used in adult DCD research (Cousins & Smyth task set). — [Lancaster](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/18770)
- **Interpreting fitness tests.** Fitness test results in people with DCD may be confounded by coordination. — [Hands 2013](https://researchonline.nd.edu.au/health_conference/38)
- **Cue responsiveness.** A verbal correction cue changes sagittal angles in hypermobile children, which shows that part of standing alignment is "postural" (adjustable) rather than structural. — [PMC5237459](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5237459/)

### Inferences
**A practical discrimination scheme** for the report. Each item is an inference from the findings above, not a validated protocol:
1. **Hypermobility pathway.** Beighton score plus 5PQ, and a note of pain or skin signs. A high score makes the "connective tissue" explanation more likely for sway-back, knee hyperextension, standing intolerance and peripheral joint pain. A low score largely rules it out. [moderate basis]
2. **Persistent motor coordination pathway.** ADC (childhood and current sections). Ideally also a clinician-administered adult motor test, e.g. by an occupational therapist or physiotherapist. Current deficits support the claim that "motor learning and control difficulties persist". They do not by themselves show that alignment was caused by them. [moderate basis]
3. **Structural vs habitual alignment.** If the "bad posture" corrects fully when cued or with lying or supported testing, it is largely habitual or capacity-related. If it persists, e.g. high lordosis with high pelvic incidence on an X-ray already taken for other reasons, or fixed kyphosis, it is more likely morphological or genetic, and childhood motor problems become a less likely main cause. Imaging solely for this purpose is not recommended by this note. [speculative application of the pelvic-incidence and heritability findings]
4. **Fitness and body composition pathway.** Endurance tests (e.g. trunk extensor and flexor holds), BMI and waist, and current activity level. These are the most *modifiable* links in the motor competence → activity → BMI → posture chain. [moderate basis for links; speculative for the individual]
5. **Mood and pain-processing pathway.** Depression, anxiety and catastrophising are strong mediators of ADHD-related pain and are associated with slumped posture. They should be assessed before attributing pain to posture. [moderate basis]
6. **Family resemblance as a crude genetic check.** If parents or siblings share a similar posture or body shape, that points toward heritable morphology. [speculative, informed by twin data]

**Overall verdict on the user's hypothesis.**
- **For:** persistence of motor problems is common; lower activity in adulthood; higher BMI; more hypermobility in clinical ADHD; reduced proprioception in DCD and in hypermobility. Together these provide several plausible *indirect* routes from childhood ADHD/DCD to adult body shape.
- **Against:**
  - No study directly links childhood DCD or ADHD to adult static posture.
  - Static posture has large genetic and morphological components.
  - Static posture is weakly and inconsistently related to pain.
  - The hypermobility link disappears outside clinical samples.
  - "Major cause" cannot be verified for one person.
- **A defensible framing:** "probably a contributor through activity, fitness and motor-skill pathways, and possibly through hypermobility; the size of its contribution is unknowable; the modifiable parts (capacity, strength, endurance, motor skill, BMI, pain beliefs) are the same whether or not ADHD caused them."

### Gaps
- **The age-9 "MRI showing slow hand development" needs clarification from the user.** Delayed skeletal maturation is normally assessed by a hand–wrist *X-ray* (bone age), not an MRI, and fine-motor delay is a different construct; this distinction is my inference and was not researched. The report should not interpret this finding without the original record.
- No validated individual-level causal attribution method exists for developmental history → adult posture.
- Normative adult values and minimal detectable change for the ADC and Beighton score were not retrieved.
- Beighton cut-offs by age and sex were not verified.
- **Search coverage was cut short.** These planned searches were not run because the budget ran out:
  - Gothenburg motor and health outcomes at age 16 (Hellgren 1993)
  - Kaiser 2015 review of motor problems in ADHD
  - ADHD postural-control meta-analysis
  - Adolescent low motor competence → back pain
  - Beighton criteria
  - Exercise effects on kyphosis and lordosis
